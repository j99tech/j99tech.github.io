## Getting here

![A220-300](https://i.ibb.co/MmVGFCJ/IMG-5269.jpg)

The trains seem very slow from Riga to Tallinn. I'm not sure my dad was keen on another bus, so that left flying as the only other option. It's only a 50 minute flight, but there's hours of shite around it, so it's similar in duration to getting the bus. Getting to the airport could have been smoother - we got caught in a thunderstorm and the first bus simply drove past us. We went to another stop and caught the next one 15 mins later. Riga airport is pretty nice. No security queue at all, so I used the waiting time to do the Riga blog post. The flight is run by Air Baltic and costs around £40 if you book it early. Their cheapest tier only allows a bag depth of 15cm, so you have to upgrade one level to get to Ryanair/Wizzair sizes. The plane was pretty new, half empty and nice and clean. There was even free Starlink wifi.

## Stuff we did
<!-- excerpt -->
On arrival, we could immediately check in, so we pretty much just got the bus from the airport straight to the apartment. Football wasn't far off starting, so after the usual food shop and unpacking of stuff/settling, we were back to watching the world cup.

![Steve](https://i.ibb.co/h1VjQByr/IMG-5277.jpg)

My dad had picked a couple of things he wanted to see on our first full day, so I crafted a walking route based on those. The route started with a viewpoint over the old town, where we met [Steven/Stephen (just coming!) the seagull](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pknVJ5oWQjarW9Yp7). We passed through the market on the way to the wooden building district and then onto the harbour for the [maritime museum](https://meremuuseum.ee/en/). It's a converted seaplane harbour hangar that's clearly had plenty spent on it. Although the admission initially feels a bit steep at 22€, it's actually fine by the time you've seen everything. There's a WW2 submarine to explore in the hangar and a couple of other ships in the harbour that your ticket gets you on to. The next stop was the abandoned Linnahall building, which was constructed to host the sailing events of the Moscow '80 olympics, but appears to have been left to die since. However, I think the dilapidated exterior presentation perhaps masks a much better preserved interior, as it was still in use about 15 years ago. We walked back via the promenade by the ferry port and a few other monuments and historic buildings. It was a long walk, so took pretty much all day to cover the 12 miles.

![Dead building](https://i.ibb.co/9H5KGCTS/IMG-5297.jpg)

I didn't want another long walking day for our second full day, so it was either take the ferry to spend the day on Aegna, or take a bit of a trip outside of Tallinn so at least some of the day could be spent sat travelling. In the end, we decided the amount of time you have to spend before the return ferry on Aegna was perhaps too much, and many of the main excursions would take a bit too long. So, we took a 20ish minute bus journey out to Pirita. There's a nice stretch of beach to walk along, and some lovely woodland to then explore. In the woods is what seems to be an estate of abandoned buildings that are pretty easy to access. There's a decent chance we shouldn't really have been there, but I think they get plenty of people visiting. We ate our sandwiches at the velodrome, which is apparently still in use, despite looking really uncomfortable. The yacht harbour then allowed us to complete our Moscow '80 Olympics relic tour, with the olympic logo sign and the olympic flame (which was actually lit). We didn't bother going in to the convent ruins, as it seemed a bit of a cash grab to charge for entry when you can see the bulk from outside anyway.

## Accommodation

![Bedroom view](https://i.ibb.co/RpK5NZbh/IMG-5271.jpg)

We had a [top-floor flat in the old town](https://www.booking.com/hotel/ee/cosy-apartment-in-old-town.en-gb.html). It's fair to call it compact - the lounge and kitchen are a single room, and the bathroom had a quirky layout to fit a bath and toilet in. However, the bedrooms were still fine, and the place was thoughtfully equipped. At only £60ish per night, it's hard to question the value given the location. It's supposed to be for up to 6 people, but I think 4 would probably be tripping over each other. No reason not to recommend. The photo is the view from the bedroom window.

## Food

![Burger](https://i.ibb.co/LFdWTYY/IMG-5286.jpg)

Sadly no nearby Lidl, so it was over to Prisma in the old town for provisions. Still no sign of potatoes, so I opted for some eggs and mushrooms to fry an omelette. As ever, the brevity of the stay combined with the quantities of food you're forced to buy meant I'd be having the same another night. The prices were higher again than Latvia, although I didn't have a Lidl to directly compare them, so it could just have been old town tax. Had my first meal that someone else cooked by getting a burger from the sports bar. It was pretty decent, and at 14€ I didn't feel ripped off.

## Footy

![Big screen](https://i.ibb.co/Q30hBktw/IMG-5272.jpg)

The first place we've stayed where the TV isn't good enough to use. A small TV thats about 6ft off the ground with a dodgy remote and no aerial doesn't really offer anything over our laptop/ipad options. So, we used a local sports bar; the Unibet Sports Bar in the old town, to watch the earlier kick-offs. It's the sort of sports bar where you can't look anywhere without seeing a big screen, but it also had a relaxed atmosphere. They even do half time and full time analyses, and a score prediction competition.

## Gaming pick

![Bonk](https://i.ibb.co/4RvRcFDB/Screenshot-2026-06-23-at-16-09-20.png)

On the short flight over, I took a look through the PC Engine library for some inspration. I was familiar with Bonk from playing BC Kid many years ago on the Amiga, so I picked Bonk's Revenge. It's a cartoony platformer. The controls felt a bit unresponsive, which is frustrating when you hit sections that need precision. I played through the easiest mode. Like many games of the time, the easy mode doesn't give you the full game. In the case of Bonk's Revenge, it only really gets you to the end of the first boss. Not even the normal difficulty gets you the full game - just expert! It passed a bit of time, but I don't think it's worth revisiting.

## Total spend

At least **£80**. Watching footy at the bar comes with quite high costs - I figure it's only fair to have a drink per half to pay for the privilege. There was also the burger. The maritime museum wasn't too cheap. Each journey on public transport costs 2€, which is the most expensive we've seen so far on the trip.

## Was it good?

It's another nice city. While we've now seen our fair share of old towns, the one in Tallinn feels like it has the best balance of presentation and vibrancy. I suppose the city does lack a bit of its own character, though. Outside of the old town, it feels mostly like you could be in any capital.
