## Baltics '26 - Warsaw

## Getting here

The plan was to start the trip in Lithuania, but there's no direct flights there from north/midlands airports. I looked at what other options there were, and saw that WizzAir fly to Warsaw from Leeds Bradford for very little money. So, for a mere £18 each, this was the option we went for. It was an early flight, so we stayed overnight at my sister's in Leeds and handily got a lift to the airport in the morning. 

Wizzair left a great impression - a modern, clean A321 Neo, and no real care for baggage restrictions (we didn't see anyone's bags being checked). Better than recent experiences with RyanAir. Once we hit Warsaw, we were held on the plane for quite a long time while they allocated some buses. Unfortunately, there was a system failure at border control, making it take around 10 minutes per person. It took a couple of hours from landing to getting into the country proper.

## Stuff we did

![Dystopia garden](https://i.ibb.co/wFQW1XRR/ghiblidad.jpg)

Due to the border issues, we had reduced time on the first day, mainly taking the opportunity to get our bearings by walking to the old town. On a lazy wander to Lidl, we checked out the river and took a small detour to the University Library Rooftop Garden, which was excellent - think Ghibli apocalypse or The Last of Us. The old town is an odd place, as it's not actually old. It was flattened in conflict and reconstructed, so it feels a bit like a theme park, although it's still pleasant.

![Museum in the park](https://i.ibb.co/spLPNdNj/IMG-5210.jpg)

The second day saw us take a short bus trip over to the Royal Lazienki Museum grounds; an impressive park that's really nice to wander around on a sunny day. We walked back to the old town through Nowy Swiat and a few more bits of park. The weather remained great, which made it a lovely way to get an impression of a decent chunk of the city. We finished the day watching the light and music show at the nearby multimedia fountain park. It has some nice laser projections, and was a worthwhile interruption during the footy.

![Praga dead flats](https://i.ibb.co/Fb37SGp2/IMG-5215.jpg)

On the final day, we headed over to the Praga district. There were a couple of heavy showers, but the weather was otherwise fine. The place is an interesting mix of pre-war, run down and often abandoned buildings juxtaposed with modern developments. Our walk ended with a path alongside the river and a trip over the recently built foot/cycle bridge back toward the old town, both offering good views across the city. Later in the day, we decided to get a different perspective by vising the observation deck on the 'syringe' (palace of culture and science); a building that was a gift to to city from the Soviet Union, therefore isn't well-loved. The view is fairly underwhelming, to be honest.

## Accommodation

Our apartment was in a great location towards the edge of the walled old town. Quiet but central, which is what you want. The place was clean and reasonably spacious. It had some recent mixed reviews - booking fairly late meant we had to make some concessions or spend a lot. It was entirely fine, but needed some attention to detail to take it to excellent. It cost [about £100 per night via AirBnB](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/rooms/1396226525760070431).

The problem with a lot of private apartment rentals is that people don't use them for cooking, despite there being a kitchen. This tends to mean a poor range of utensils and equipment. In this case, there were no tea towels or oven gloves, along with a lack of some kitchen basics. So, if you cook something on the hob (there's no oven), you've got nothing to pick it up/move it with. Similarly, if you want to wash the pots, there's nothing to dry them with. Tiny towel to the rescue!

## Food

Unsurprisingly (to anyone that knows me), I kept things cheap. I made sandwiches for lunch and cooked for evening meals. I had a varying combinations of polish sausage, nduja, cheese pierogi, gnocchi, pasta sauce and jalepenos. It's fair to say it's not really an ideally balanced diet, so I'm hoping increased cooking options will help in the next city.

## Footy

![The TV](https://i.ibb.co/W4ZpXYMW/IMG-5206.jpg)

The World Cup is broadcast free on Polish terrestrial TV, so we just used that. The opener with Mexico vs South Africa was decent enough, mainly thanks to the red card spree. I watched the South Korea vs Czechia game on catchup the next morning, which felt like it could have gone either way. The Canada opener was pretty entertaining, as was the USA one. Qatar v Switzeland was the last match we could realistically watch. It's going to be difficult to catch up from this point due to the travel and the increase in frequency of matches.

## Gaming pick

![The real impact of mowing down everything in your path](https://i.ibb.co/CKy3HYjB/Screenshot-2026-06-12-at-19-40-45.png)

On the flight out, I fired up the Miyoo and took a look through the PS1 collection. I'd heard of Harmful Park before, but had no idea what it was. I think it was a Japan exclusive that has an English fan translation. Turns out it's a horizonal shooter based around a theme park with some crazy enemy design and a lot of character. I played it on easy and failed to get through it, but really enjoyed my time with it. I'm not sure I figured out all the mechanics, but there's 4 selectable, independently upgradeable weapons, each with their own special. Zany fun that I plan to return to.

## Total spend

I reckon I spent around **£40** in Warsaw. The viewpoint at the syringe was the only activity we did that had an admission fee, so it was mostly food and drink.

## Was it good?

Yeah, it seems a really nice city. Lots of green space and wide boulevards. Good, cheap transport. People seem polite, and it doesn't feel a particularly hectic. Plenty of variety and a recommended place to visit for a few days.
