## Baltics '26 - Kaunas

## Getting here

![Train compartment](https://i.ibb.co/kgZZnPLt/IMG-5226.jpg)

We decided to get the train. I figured it might be a good way to see the country, but it takes a fair while - 6.5h including the need to switch trains after the border. The tickets can be booked through https://www.intercity.pl/ and vary in price (rather than being fixed). There's only one viable train, but the timing isn't too bad. You can take it straight through to Vilnius for an extra hour of travel. I booked a compartment seat, as it was the only way to face the direction of travel, and it turned out we had the compartment to ourselves.

It cost us about £15 each. The journey wouldn't take so long if the train didn't sit in the stations for 10 minutes, but it was comfortable enough that the time on the train didn't really matter that much. The scenery was mainly flat, empty fields that reminded me of being plonked in the middle of nowhere in Lithuania on Geoguessr (even when we were still in Poland).

## Stuff we did
<!-- excerpt -->
![Kaunas](https://i.ibb.co/5h3sCQs7/IMG-5228.jpg)

A couple of things stood out when we arrived. The first was the lack of pedestrian support to get across two 6-lane roads - you have to either Frogger them or you're walking a long way to an underpass or crossing. The second was the price increase from Poland. We headed to the supermarket (Iki), where prices were generally higher than the UK; about double Poland. We had an hour spare before the footy, so took a wander over the river and up to the Aleksotas observation point to get the lay of the land. There was a robot lawnmower making a hardy attempt at maintaining the steep grass bank. It was sunny, but the ominous dark cloud behind us caught up on the way back and required some haste (and sheltering).

![Bunker](https://i.ibb.co/Y42tzqWB/IMG-5230.jpg)

We didn't have much planned for the next day, so I emailed [the Atomic Bunker museum](https://www.atominisbunkeris.lt/en/) at night to see if they could accommodate us (you have to book). I didn't expect a response at 11:30pm! We arrived at the time they told us, and it turned out all museums are shut on Mondays, so they'd opened it up for us and a few other brits especially. This meant we didn't get an English speaking guide, instead an enthusiastic Lithuanian guy who basically pointed at everything and said "KGB! Original!". There's an overwhelming collection of stuff that spans several decades. Pretty interesting and also a bit quirky. €12 each.

![Amazing word](https://i.ibb.co/jkZwVf30/IMG-5232.jpg)

The new knowledge of museums being closed meant we abandoned the plan to visit the 9th Fort. Instead, we took the bus back in to town and followed the clusters of 'attractions' on Google Maps. The first was an underpass, to give you an idea of the calibre of attraction we're talking about. The route took us up plenty of steps and through the park, before returning back to the old town via the abandoned, unfinished hotel (which has the longest word this side of rural Wales emblazoned upon it)and the very long main shopping boulevard. We saw some light rain at points, but it was generally fine until the final half mile, where the rain got heavy.

## Accommodation

We used booking.com for [an apartment in the old town](https://www.booking.com/hotel/lt/kurpiu-gatve.en-gb.html), just off the main street. It was quiet, spacious and clean. It was better equipped than the Warsaw place and, at about £60 per night, was very good value. As is usually the case when you book a place for 2 people, the assumption was that we would be sharing a bed, so only one was made up. That's a bit of a pain. The washing machine is a Miele, which excited me probably a little too much. There wasn't anywhere obvious to dry clothes, though. No reason not to recommend it. 

## Food

Annoyingly, while the internet connection had around 250mbps capacity, it was wildly inconsistent, which was a problem for uninterrupted live streaming. The games were broadcast on Lithuanian TV, but the channel that did so was SD. Better than nothing, but not ideal. Some good games with some good goals, but at this point I'm watching more as highlights than in full. Turkiye; my sweepstake team, are winning me nothing!

## Gaming pick

![GT2 tough B-4](https://i.ibb.co/qFcq8hSY/Screenshot-2026-06-15-at-17-21-30.png)

I decided to fire up Gran Turismo 2 for some of the train journey over. The licence tests did the trick of passing some time, even if getting gold on B-4 was more difficult than it really should be. It's fun to look back at a game where, at the time, we were certain graphics couldn't possibly get better than this. It's looking pretty dated now, but still plays alright, even if a digital d-pad makes it tough.

## Total spend

About **£30**. Food, bus travel and the bunker museum.

## Was it good?

We were only really in Kaunas for one full day, but it feels like there's not a huge amount more to see. It's fair to say it's quite a contrast to Warsaw, which feel a lot more pedestrian and tourist friendly. However, it still feels a nice place to be. The shopping street feels like it belongs in a far larger city, so it's quite peculiar to find it in Kaunas.
