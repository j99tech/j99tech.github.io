## Getting here

The only train from Vilnius is at about 7am, so we opted for a midday coach instead. It's a 4 hour journey. To try to make it more bearable, we went with [Lux Express](https://luxexpress.eu/en/); one of the more premium operators, and opted for their 'Lounge' upgraded seats. To be honest, I'd have happily just gone with the standard seats, as the price was quite an increase and it didn't feel like too much of a difference. It took 4 hours, and was generally ok, but you could feel every imperfection in the road surface (and Lithuanian roads have many of them), so I never felt particularly comfortable. The wifi was also unusable, so I used a few gigs tethering.

## Stuff we did
<!-- excerpt -->
![The most basic interpretation of Donkey](https://i.ibb.co/LzbFwFLd/IMG-5246.jpg)

After stepping out of the bus station, Riga immediately felt more like a big city than either of the Lithuanian cities. It was bustling, quite busy with tourists (which Kaunas definitely wasn't, and Vilnius didn't have many), and had many long, wide roads packed with buildings. Apparently Vilnius and Riga have a similar population, but you wouldn't know when comparing the two. We didn't have much time before the first kick-off, so just had a quick walk around and a trip to Lidl (I now have a Plus account in 4 countries...) to stock up.

![Riga panorama](https://i.ibb.co/7J0Xt0hC/IMG-5262.jpg)

The weather was lovely throughout. On day 2, we went for a fairly lengthy walk away from the centre, which didn't really have a lot to offer other than a mixture of old and newer properties. In the centre, we looked at the art nouveau buildings, then took a boat trip. It took in a canal through a park before heading to the main river. The guy at the helm (Oleg) offered his own guidance in preference to the pre-recorded audio track. He did a great job of covering the main sights and history over the course of the 50 minute trip. For a fiver it was well worth it (cheaper to book through a third party online than directly at the dock). As the first match didn't kick off until 10pm, we headed up the St Peter's Church tower when the sun was pretty low. The viewpoint was much better than the viewing platform in Warsaw - there's quite a lot more to see. Worth the £8 entry.

![Fox doesn't like the soviet tower](https://i.ibb.co/NndTXRt6/IMG-5264.jpg)

Our final day saw us walk over to the markets - a striking set of buildings, partially constructed from old WW1 zeppelin hangars. There was a vast outdoor market on top of the pretty huge indoor space. Walking back along the river, we decided to cross over to the other side. We passed through Victory Park and took the next bridge up back over. After seeing the "Three Brothers" historic buildings and "Cat House", we figured we'd struck off most tourist sights, so took a tram over to the Zoo. I'm not one to appraise the suitability of the enclosures for the animals, so I've no idea if it was adequate, but I thought the Zoo was decent. Passed about 3 hours. I managed to find a random discount code, so it was about £11 each, which felt good value. Because I can't let Lidl Plus freebies go unclaimed, I nipped back there to claim said freebies with partial success.

## Accommodation

I opted for a central location, as people said that the old town is quite noisy. We've seen a few stag groups, so I think it's probably a fair judgement. We were just off a main road that was fairly quiet at night. [The apartment](https://www.booking.com/hotel/lv/elegants-dzivoklis-rigas-centra.en-gb.html) was on the 5th floor (no lift). The bedrooms were huge, as was the living room. That's a bit odd when the kitchen and bathroom/toilet are quite small (the tiny toilet was like being banished to some sort of contemplation chamber). It was well furnished, and the owner left us water, wine and fruit. Another good choice, and decent value at £90 a night.

## Food

I fancied cooking something in the oven. Potatoes don't really seem to be much of a thing here, so I ended up baking a sweet potato. I went for some sausages (stuff that isn't just a frankfurter derivative seems hard to come by) and some veg. I'm slightly ashamed to admit the next couple of nights I stooped to oven wedges and I tried some Latvian baked beans (which were watery, but ok). I'm hoping I find some creativity in Estonia.

## Footy

![TV in the apartment](https://i.ibb.co/Zz8rJMWH/IMG-5247.jpg)

No aerial, so no domestic TV again. There was an IPTV box, although everything on the TV and box was set to Russian, so I have no idea if we could have made use of it. BBC and ITV via my laptop and HDMI again. As usual, we watched the earliest games and generally just did the highlights for the others. I did watch the whole Scotland match on catch-up, though. The Japan game had a 7am kick-off, so was a rare opportunity to watch another game live. Still continues to be an entertaining tournament.

## Gaming pick

![Heavy Smash](https://i.ibb.co/9mrQWWw4/Screenshot-2026-06-21-at-12-21-16.png)

I didn't spend much time gaming on the bus - I watched one of the World Cup games from the previous night. I did fire up the Miyoo at one point, though, and selected a random arcade game. Taking place in the distant future of 2010, Heavy Smash is a sort of bloodsport Handball somewhere between Speedball and Super Sidekicks. I had no idea what I was doing, but the game was pretty forgiving to start with, so had about 20 minutes of entertainment from it. Doubt I'll play it again.

## Total spend

Around **£50**. The Zoo and the St Peter's viewpoint took the majority of that, with food and a bit of public transport on top. Having visited Lidl in Lithuania and Latvia, I could compare prices directly, and everything was at least 10% higher in Latvia.

## Was it good?

Yeah, it's a lovely city. It joins Warsaw in the stand-outs that I'd be happy to visit again.
