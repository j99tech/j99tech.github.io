## Getting here

It's a 1-hour train journey from Kaunas to Vilnius, for about a tenner. We were on the 'Pink Soup' carriage, which is basically just very pink inside. Not much to say - it ran to time and was comfortable.

## Stuff we did
<!-- excerpt -->
![Rainy Vilnius](https://i.ibb.co/B2WtMwG1/IMG-5234.jpg)

We had a couple of hours to kill between our arrival and check-in, so took a bit of a wander around the centre and up to the Gediminas Castle for the view over the city. Rain was never too far away, so we also had to shelter a bit. As we headed over to the accommodation (a bit early), we bumped into the owner (we weren't expecting to meet them), who let us in to the flat a bit earlier. After a food shop, we headed to the Uzupis district and 'hill park'. There were a series of metal steps to places of interest, but only one worth taking to the 3 Crosses monument and viewpoint. The rain hit again (impressively heavily at times) and we headed back to the flat.

On the second day, we partook in our first group tour at the Lukiškės Prison. It's a closed prison (apparently not feasible to renovate) that's partially been taken over as an art and music space. There were 9 of us for an English language tour, which covered the main prison areas. The guide was decent, and the tour fairly insightful if you don't see the inside of a prison very often. Unless you're a particular fan of prisons, I wouldn't say it was essential - see one cell, see them all. On the way over there, we stopped off at the Museum of Occupation and Freedom Fights (one of the several ways it's translated). It's a text-heavy chronological path through Lithuania's history of occupation, concluding with some KGB holding cells. It's decent for an overview of the past century in Lithuania, and doesn't cost much.

![Sunnier view](https://i.ibb.co/4nz0S0DY/IMG-5242.jpg)

After the prison tour, we took a wander over to the more built up area of Vilnius. The Swedbank terrace was a nice place to stroll through, and gave a nice view over the river. We took the pedestrian/cycling bridge back towards the old town and the flat. I took a bit of a nap to give me a chance of staying awake for the England game.

## Accommodation

![Old town](https://i.ibb.co/nM7Rg8RD/IMG-5243.jpg)

It was handy that we bumped into Edita at the flat, as the second (futon) bed wasn't made up and needed some bedding. I'll be warning every place from now on that we'll be using 2 beds. [The flat itself](https://www.booking.com/hotel/lt/central-apartment-vilnius-vilnius.en-gb.html) was practically brand new, and felt a bit like a hotel room. Everything was well thought through, and an umbrella came in use on a couple of occasions. I suspect it'll be the best place we stay - great location, very clean and well-specced. The photo is the view from the tiny balcony.

## Food

Thanks to a pretty big Lidl, I stocked up on decent food and cooked pesto chicken with rice for my evening meals. The best food I've cooked so far. Also courtesy of Lidl, I tried a Kibinai; a Lithuanian version of a cornish pasty, I suppose. Nothing remarkable.

## Footy

No aerial, so we relied on the wifi and VPN to stream. Fortunately, it was solid enough. As usual, we watched the games before midnight, including the England match. Some great goals so far, and very few dull games. I've yet to see any clear public gatherings to watch the world cup at the places we've been to so far.

## Gaming pick

Apart from chipping away at a little more GT2 on the train over, I haven't used the Miyoo. Sorry!

## Total spend

Around £45. The prison tour was fairly expensive. We didn't need to use any public transport in the city, though, so that saved a bit. The rest was mainly food.

## Was it good?

It's a nice city, without having anything specific to recommend, really. It's just a pleasant place to walk around and explore. Fairly small for a capital with a laid back feel. A couple of days feels enough to get a impression of the place. We're getting quite used to walking cobbled streets at this point.
