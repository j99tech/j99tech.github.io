## Baltics '26 - Helsinki

## Getting here

![Finlandia Ferry](https://i.ibb.co/WWz76s4R/IMG-5298.jpg)

The obvious way to Helsinki from Tallinn is by ferry. They run a few times a day from 3 operators, with durations varying from 2h to 2h45. In theory, the cheapest is Viking Line, but discount codes made Eckero Line the cheapest for us, with a journey time of 2h15 for just under £15 each. The Finlandia is the largest ship I've ever been on, with a capacity of over 2000. We took a seat in one of the bar areas (pretty much the only places you can sit) and took in a very comfortable, chilled out journey. The wifi was ok at times, so I watched a bit of Pluribus. There's no real perception of movement. It's quite odd.

## Stuff we did
<!-- excerpt -->
Our AirBnB wasn't supposed to allow check-in until 5pm, but they accommodated us doing it whenever we wanted after we landed in the port (which was a little after 2pm). We took a tram for a few minutes, got ourselves settled after some initial confusion finding the apartment (Finnish street numbering nonsense). The local Lidl wasn't far, so we stocked up. We took a bit of a walk around the nearby area, and I noticed a flyer posted on a wall for what seemed to be a World Cup Fan Park. After a bit of looking online, it looked promising, and we finished the day by watching the footy there.

![Triumphant Sausage](https://i.ibb.co/yBnqVGGS/IMG-5306.jpg)

Without a specific destination targeted for our first full day, I saw my final opportunity for a mega walk. This would be the most time-consuming route so far, requiring a lot of scouring Reddit to find interesting places and then using Garmin to plot a route through them. The attractions peaked early, with a golden statue of a triumphant sausage. We passed a few less interesting statues and cut through some parks to get to the Eläintarhanlahti bay, where we walked along the edge of the water. 

![Street Art](https://i.ibb.co/8Dz3nd0r/IMG-5310.jpg)

We continued through the 1952 olympic complex (after a diversion to see a tiny hydroelectric generator) on the way over to East Pasila. This district is home to [around 100 pieces of street art](https://www.helsinkiurbanart.com/en/pasilas-street-art-on-map-2/) by talented artists from around the globe. You have to weave your way around the streets to discover the various pieces. 

![Lammassaari reeds](https://i.ibb.co/Df65sHkQ/IMG-5315.jpg)

The ultimate destination was Lammassaari - a couple of small islands with woodland and huts that are connected via boardwalks over marshland. It feels quite unique, and really added to the variety of the walk. As we were on the doorstep, we then visited the Technology Museum on pay-what-you-want Thursday. It wasn't great, and isn't worth trip to see it. I was glad for a reduced price. Because we'd already covered about 12 miles, we got the tram back.

![Suomenlinna](https://i.ibb.co/4ZxCYpGK/IMG-5323.jpg)

We had a steady final day; taking our time to leave the accommodation and then going straight to the central station to make use of the left luggage lockers. After a very small bit of souvenir shopping, we took the ferry (which is considered part of the Helsinki transport system) to Suomenlinna. It's a set of fortified islands, and is one of the key attractions of Helsinki. While it's nice to walk around, it's also rammed with other tourists,. We had a bit of time to spare once we returned, so nipped in the Helsinki City Museum. It's free, but not bad for a quick look around. It also has excellent toilets and a tall tap from which to fill your bottles. After that, it was off to the airport to begin the megaslog back home.

## Accommodation

It wasn't easy to find a decent place at a reasonable price. The AirBnB we got was free for only the two nights we needed for the entire month. The location in the 'design district' was close enough to the action. There was only one bedroom, but also a double bed in the living room. Again, it was clean and presentable, and had what we needed. Perhaps not the level of attention to detail of some of the previous places we stayed. I'll not say where the keys were stashed, but let's say it's a novel, pretty insecure approach that we also saw in Tallinn. Although the apartment was a few floors up, there was a modern lift this time to save the legs.

## Food

![Bastard](https://i.ibb.co/7x5bP8fd/IMG-5325.jpg)

As ever, Lidl did the job. This time, they had some reasonable looking new potatoes, so I combined those with some breaded pollock and mixed peppers for a couple of meals. I tried Liquorice ice creams (like cornettos), which were a bit odd, but sort of worked. I had a Bastard Burger as a bit of an end of holiday treat. I suppose it wasn't too far off Five Guys. Decent fast food burgers, fries and shakes. Very expensive for what it was, but I knew what I was getting myself into.

## Footy

![Fanzone](https://i.ibb.co/LzgKYcMT/IMG-5302.jpg)

As mentioned, we used the Coca-Cola sponsored Fanzone on the first night. It had a couple of huge screens, benches and deck chairs, and a generally great atmosphere. We got some deck chairs in a great position. Unfortunately, the next night had the earliest kick-off at 11pm, so it felt a bit too much of a stretch to go again. In fact, with an expected sleepless night expected the next day, I didn't watch any live football at all. Sleep was too important to skip. 6 games is a lot to catch up on, even if you're just watching the highlights. The overnight flights left us with a fairly long transfer in Warsaw, so I was actually able to watch a couple of the early morning games while we waited.

## Gaming pick

Failed to play anything, I'm afraid.

## Total spend

Around **£80**. Helsinki is not cheap. Using Lidl again helps with the comparison - some prices were double or more than Warsaw for the same products. The public transport is also more expensive at 3.50€ per trip. We didn't spend much on attractions, and the fanzone was free, so we controlled our spending pretty well. I imagine things could spiral if you want to eat out for most meals and shop a lot. I bought a burger on the last night (how can Bastard John resist Bastard Burgers?!), which was about £26 for a meal.

## Was it good?

It's a lovely city. I imagine it's a great place to live, provided you're happy living in a flat, and your salary scales to the cost of living. As a tourist destination, I'm not so sure. It's expensive and doesn't necessarily have a wealth of standout attractions.

Warsaw and Tallinn both have Chopin benches that play heavily distorted Chopin at the press of a button. I can't figure out why Helsinki doesn't have a Bomfunk Bench in a Metro station, or Sandstorm Seat in Senate Square. Surely these better anything Chopin ever penned. Finland needs to celebrate these modern masters!

## How we're getting home

You can fly directly to Manchester from Helsinki, but I never saw it drop below £200. It's cheaper to fly to a southern airport, but that just gives you cost and more travel hours at the other end. I found a wallet-friendly, but challenging, alternative. The flight back to Leeds from Warsaw with Wizzair is still cheap. Ryanair does a flight to Warsaw from Helsinki for about £35, but it goes to the other Warsaw airport (Modlin) and lands over 6 hours before the Wizzair flight. Transferring between airports takes some of that time, at least.

This solved the problem with the place we stayed only being available for 2 nights, saves the cost of another night by travelling overnight, and cuts the flight cost in more than half. Of course, the problem is we have to do something with our bags all day on the final day, and there's likely to be a lot of waiting and not much sleeping. Was the trade-off worth it? Depends to what lengths you're willing to go save money, I guess. Everything wen smoothly, but it's a long time to get from A to B.

## Thoughts on the trip as a whole

I had a great time. I always think you get out what you put in. There's no doubt a lot of planning went into the trip, and then on what to do while we were away. I think all the planning removed any pressure during the trip to make any big decisions. I didn't feel we wasted any days. We had a couple of slightly more relaxed days, but otherwise I'm really not sure there's more we could have done. We walked over 100 miles. There's no doubt it was relentless - it's a holiday to explore rather than relax. My dad handled the demands exceptionally well. Being able to keep up with the World Cup was also a bonus, and didn't get in the way due to the timing of the games.

While some of the cities left a stronger impression, I thought they were all worth visiting. Warsaw was perhaps the biggest surprise. It was added to the list more out of convenience. The impression I got from some Reddit threads was that it's not an interesting place. In fact, we thought it was a great city. It's also very cheap and easy to get to, and pretty low cost while you're there.

Something that all places had in common was how they highlighted the nobhead problem in the UK. The number of free public toilets in Warsaw, the fleets of robot lawnmowers maintaining public places in Kaunas, the various personal transport rental options everywhere, the unscathed art installations in Helsinki. It's simply not fathomable that these things could exist in the UK. Portaloos would be tipped and torched. The mowers would be nicked or vandalised within 24 hours. The attempt to try rental bikes in Sheffield resulted in them being claimed as trophies by lads from bad estates. Any public art displays would likely be defaced.

## Total overall spend

I reckon it was around **£1250** for 16 days (all travel, accommodation, food, local transport, entry fees, insurance).
